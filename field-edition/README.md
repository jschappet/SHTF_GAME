# Cultivating Resilience Field Edition

**Three to seven days in person.** Live at a host site, share meals and camp work, and build things the next group can use. Start with three bounties: a phone charging station, a weather shelter, and a water filtration station. Each bounty ends with someone from another crew operating the finished build.

## Set up the camp

Players organize and fund their own camp. Agree together on who will host, what each person is willing to bring or do, and how to cover shared expenses.

- The host confirms site permission, sleeping arrangements, toilets and handwashing, a reliable supply of drinking water, and an indoor or off-site fallback. Walk the site together on arrival and show everyone how to reach the host and leave camp.
- Keep emergency communications and ordinary access to food, drinking water, and shelter available throughout. Apply the power-out premise to the projects. Players use off-grid power for the charging bounty while emergency phones stay available.
- Put the three bounty cards on a board. For each, name a build lead, a second crew that will run the test, and the person who will tend the finished project. Small groups can build in sequence. Make meals, cleanup, and rest part of the daily plan.
- Inventory what players can bring, share, or borrow. List any remaining supplies and site costs, agree on a spending limit and how to split expenses before anyone buys anything, and track what was spent. Scale the builds to the resources the group can commit.

## Run of play

| Day | Work on site |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | Walk the site, make camp, share a meal, and inventory what you brought. Choose build locations, sketch each build, and agree on how it will be tested. Start assembly while there is daylight. |
| 2 | Build and run first tests. Log what failed, what needs a different part, and what another crew could help with. Use the shelter as a daytime gathering spot once the host approves it. |
| 3 | Have a different crew run each acceptance test. Repair and retest where possible. For a three-day session, finish with a shared meal and handoff. |
| 4–7, if staying | Use and measure the builds over several days. Rotate operators, test weak points under ordinary site conditions, and make one improvement to each station. On the last day, repeat the tests and hand off the builds or pack them down for the next host. |

At breakfast, name what the camp needs today and who can offer help. At dinner, bring one measured result or something you made to the table. If a build fails, write down the failure and the part or change the next crew needs.

## Bounty 1 · Build a phone charging station

**Leave behind:** A portable, labeled USB charging station that can recharge off-grid.

**Build:** Use a portable solar panel and a commercially enclosed battery or power bank with manufacturer-supported charging inputs, plus compatible phone cables. Build a stable panel stand and a dry, ventilated station for the battery, cables, and sign-out log. Route cables away from foot traffic. Follow the equipment manuals for connections, weather limits, and operation.

**Earn the bounty:** A second crew connects the panel and demonstrates energy going into the battery without mains power. Using the station, they raise a compatible phone's displayed charge by at least 10 percentage points within an hour, with the phone starting below 70%. Record the starting and ending charge, elapsed time, and sky conditions. If weather delays the recharge test, record the phone test separately and leave the recharge check open.

## Bounty 2 · Build a weather shelter

**Leave behind:** A temporary rain shelter with a dry work area for four people and their gear.

**Build:** Use a tarp, compatible poles, lines, and site-appropriate anchors. Make a simple site sketch showing approach, anchor points, and where runoff goes. Choose a clear site with the host, away from overhead hazards and standing water. Mark lines that cross near foot traffic.

**Earn the bounty:** Four people can sit or work with their gear under cover and enter and leave freely. The host checks anchors, overhead clearance, and drainage before use. In ordinary rain or a gentle hose test, the work surface stays dry for ten minutes and runoff stays clear of the entrance. A second crew uses the sketch to check or retension the shelter. Use the host's sleeping fallback during severe weather or if the shelter fails.

## Bounty 3 · Build a water filtration station

**Leave behind:** A labeled gravity filtration station with a stable stand, a commercial gravity filter, a feed container, and a covered collection container.

**Build:** Use the filter manufacturer's approved fittings and setup. Make a stand that holds both containers securely, and mark the feed and collection sides so hoses and containers stay separate. Write the setup, cleaning, and storage instructions on a card at the station.

**Earn the bounty:** With known potable tap water as the test feed, a second crew assembles the station from the card and filters two liters without leaks or mixing up the two sides. Record the volume and elapsed time. Demonstrate shutdown and storage. This test measures assembly and flow. For camp drinking and cooking, use the host's verified drinking-water supply. For any future use with untreated water, follow the filter instructions and local health guidance for source selection and any additional treatment. See [CDC emergency water guidance](https://www.cdc.gov/water-emergency/about/).

## Pass it forward

Hand the host three things for each bounty: **the build or packed kit, a one-page operating card, and the test record**. Mark checks as passed or still open. Include parts used, what broke or changed, and who agreed to maintain the build.

Put a copy of each card and test record in the group's repository or portable archive. Link back to the previous generation so the next camp can rebuild, repair, or improve what you made.
