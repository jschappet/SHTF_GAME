## Phone charging station

### USB solar panel and power bank

**Gather.** A portable panel with regulated USB output, a power bank whose manufacturer supports charging from that output, compatible cables, a panel stand or kickstand, and a dry tray for the bank.

1. Match the panel's USB output and cable to the bank's supported charging input. Check the bank's indicators so the next operator can tell whether it is receiving charge.
2. Set the panel on its stand in direct sun. Put the bank in a shaded, dry, ventilated spot within cable reach. Keep cables clear of walkways and leave slack at the plugs.
3. Connect the panel to the bank's input using the supported cable. Confirm the charging indicator and record the conditions. Use separate recharge and phone charging steps unless the manual explicitly supports simultaneous operation.
4. Connect the phone to the bank's USB output. Label the input and output and pack a spare compatible phone cable if available.

### Portable power station and folding solar panel

**Gather.** An enclosed portable power station with a solar input and USB output, a manufacturer-compatible folding panel and solar cable, a stable platform, and a compatible phone cable.

1. Check the panel and station manuals for approved connectors and solar input limits, including voltage, current, and power. Use the supported combination and cable.
2. Unfold the panel on stable ground in direct sun. Place the station on a dry, shaded, ventilated platform. Route the solar cable away from feet and moving equipment.
3. Connect the panel as directed by the manufacturer. Check the station's input display or charging indicator. Start with room in the battery for incoming charge.
4. Enable the station's USB output and connect the phone. Label the solar connection and USB controls and add the setup and shutdown sequence to the operating card.

**Test either build.** Have a second crew demonstrate solar input with mains disconnected. Then charge a phone from below 70% by at least 10 percentage points within one hour. Record readings, elapsed time, and sky conditions. If there is too little sun, record the phone test and leave the solar check open.

## Weather shelter

For both pitches, start with a tarp around 12 × 16 ft and lay out four seats and their gear before placing anchors. Size the footprint to your crew. Choose a site with the host away from overhead hazards and standing water, with drainage away from the entrance. Use compatible poles and anchors suited to the ground. Take the shelter down if conditions exceed the gear's limits.

### Two-pole A-frame tarp

**Gather.** A tarp with reinforced ridge and edge points, two compatible poles, two ridge-end guylines, side lines, anchors, and visible line markers.

1. Lay out the tarp with the long dimension along the ridge and mark both open ends. Set one pole at each ridge end using the tarp's intended attachments.
2. Guy each pole outward along the ridge. With a partner holding the poles, loosely anchor both tarp sides, then tension opposite corners in pairs.
3. Set the side edges low enough to shed rain away from the work area. Adjust until both roof faces drain and the tarp has no pockets that collect water.
4. Put seats and gear inside the drip line. Mark the guylines and keep an approach beside an end guyline clear. Sketch the layout so another crew can retension it.

### Lean-to tarp with an open front

**Gather.** A tarp with reinforced corner and edge points, two compatible poles, front guylines, rear anchors, side lines as needed, and visible line markers.

1. Mark the open front facing away from prevailing rain, and leave room for water to run off behind the shelter.
2. Secure the rear edge low using its reinforced points. With a partner, raise the two front corners on the poles and guy each pole outward to stable anchors.
3. Tension the side lines as needed to make one continuous slope toward the rear. Adjust rear anchors so runoff clears the work area and surrounding paths.
4. Place the four seats well under the roof and mark the lines. Sketch the front approach and the runoff path for the next crew.

**Test either build.** Have the host check the site, clearance, and anchors. Seat four people with gear and confirm clear access. Use ordinary rain or a gentle hose test for ten minutes. Check that the work surface stays dry and runoff stays clear of the entrance. Ask a second crew to check or retension the pitch using the sketch.

## Water filtration station

Use known potable tap water for commissioning. The flow test checks setup, leaks, and handling. Use the host's verified supply for camp drinking and cooking. For future treatment of untreated water, follow the manufacturer and local health guidance on source safety and additional treatment. [CDC emergency water guidance](https://www.cdc.gov/water-emergency/about/)

### Hanging gravity filter

**Gather.** A commercial hanging gravity filter kit with a feed bag, filter, and approved hoses, a covered collection container or kit collection bag, a freestanding support rated for the full bag, and labels.

1. Set the stand on firm, level ground. Label the feed bag and inlet hose **FEED** and label the outlet hose and receiver **COLLECTION**.
2. Hang the feed bag from the rated support and place the receiver on a low, stable shelf or on the ground. Set the height difference required by the kit and follow its instructions for orienting and priming the filter.
3. Run the outlet into the covered receiver through the kit's approved connection. Keep the outlet away from the feed bag, inlet hose, and ground.
4. Fill the feed bag with test water and start the flow. Mark where to clamp or shut off the line. Add the kit's cleaning and storage steps to the operating card.

### Shelf-fed gravity filter

**Gather.** A commercial gravity filter kit designed for an elevated rigid reservoir, the approved reservoir and fittings, a sturdy two-level stand or shelves rated for full containers, a covered receiver, and labels.

1. Set the stand on firm, level ground and secure the upper reservoir against sliding or tipping. Place the receiver on the lower shelf, leaving space to remove it with the lid on. Confirm the height difference against the kit instructions.
2. Fit the kit's approved outlet, line, and cartridge as directed. Label both sides and route the hose without kinks or strain. Use the kit's covered collection connection.
3. Fill the reservoir with test water and open the valve. Prime or flush as directed by the manufacturer. Inspect connections with the reservoir loaded and while water flows.
4. Shut the valve, cap the receiver, and show where each side's equipment will be stored. Put the cleaning procedure and parts list on the operating card.

**Test either build.** Using the operating card alone, a second crew assembles the station and runs two liters of known potable tap water through it. Record time, volume, any leaks, and whether the feed and collection sides stayed separate. Have the crew demonstrate shutdown and storage.

## Add new knowledge

A useful contribution can be a full build, a repair that worked, a clarified instruction, or a field note about a failed test.

1. For a small correction, edit the relevant guide. For a new build or substantial field note, add a Markdown file in this folder with a descriptive name such as `shelter-low-ridge-field-test.md`.
2. Use the template below. Record what the crew actually observed and label any untested steps or changes. If a failure is worth keeping, include the conditions and what you would try next.
3. Add a relative link to the new file under [Crew contributions](#crew-contributions). Link back to any guide or prior generation the entry builds on.
4. On GitHub, use **Edit** for a correction or **Add file → Create new file** for a new entry. Propose the change on a branch and open a pull request, or send it to the person tending your crew's repository. With an offline copy, save the same Markdown files beside this index and include them in the next handoff.
5. Ask someone who did not write the entry to follow the instructions or read the test notes. Add what they found before calling a build field-tested.
