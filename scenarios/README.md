# Starter scenarios

Pick a scenario, read its opening aloud, and see what your group can make possible with the people and resources at hand.

## Quick start

**Players:** 3–8, including one facilitator. **Time:** 45–75 minutes. **Materials:** paper and pencils. Use a hand-drawn map for tabletop play or mark fictional locations around a familiar site for a walk-through.

1. Each player names one skill or resource they can offer and one responsibility or need they bring. Ask before assigning another character a job or committing their supplies.
2. Draw the key locations and write down the starting supplies listed in the scenario and any contributions the players agree to bring into play. Each round, every player proposes one action. Players can act together, take care of a need, gather information, or ask a neighbor for help.
3. After the first round, read the **Twist**. Play two more rounds. The facilitator describes what actions achieve and tracks time, supplies, and promises. When the outcome is uncertain, offer a concrete cost or tradeoff and let the player decide whether to proceed.
4. End by checking the bounty together. Record what worked, who still needs support, and one thing the next group can inherit. A partial result can become the opening situation for the next game.

Keep damage, closures, and outages fictional. During walk-throughs, everyone can leave the play area and use their phone at any time.

## Power Out

**Read aloud.** In 24 hours, the power goes out across the valley. Five camps in the Cascadia watershed are preparing to get through the first three days and plan for a year. You're playing one camp. Once the grid goes down, your camp has no mains power or Wi-Fi.

**Start with.** Three cars, five gallons of gasoline total, a pantry with three days of meals, paper maps, and a neighbor who can carry one message before dark. Mark the other four camps on the map.

**Rounds.** Last day with power → first night → morning of day three. After round one, close any options that depended on the grid.

**Twist.** A neighboring camp can share food but needs a dry room for two people tonight.

**Bounty.** Make a three-day plan for meals, shelter, and check-ins. Exchange a message with another camp and bring back a reply. Leave a one-year plan with three seasonal milestones, a person willing to tend each one, and the biggest unanswered question.

## Tree Falls on the House

**Read aloud.** A tree comes down through the roof during a storm. Everyone is accounted for outside. The house is closed until an assessment, and six residents need somewhere to stay.

**Start with.** A neighbor offers two beds. The community hall has four cots and needs someone to open it. One resident has a dog, and another needs a step-free place to sleep. A friend can make one supply run.

**Rounds.** First hour → dusk → next morning.

**Twist.** The hall's keyholder is away until morning. A second neighbor offers a living room if someone helps move furniture and stays to welcome people.

**Bounty.** Confirm a sleeping place for each resident and the dog, arrange the supply run, and agree on who will check in with everyone tomorrow.

## No Internet or Cellphones

**Read aloud.** Internet and mobile service stop working on market day. Three people at different sites are still expecting a delivery. The pickup time has changed, and a paper note with the old time is already circulating.

**Start with.** A bicycle, two willing messengers, a wall clock at the hall, and bulletin boards at the market, hall, and farm stand. Give each player a starting location. During rounds, exchange information only when characters meet or a written message arrives.

**Rounds.** Morning market → noon pickup → afternoon check-in. A messenger can visit one other site and return in a round.

**Twist.** The market closes early. Someone headed there has already left home.

**Bounty.** Get the corrected time and place to all three recipients, collect acknowledgments, and leave a paper message format another crew could use tomorrow.

## No Gas

**Read aloud.** The station's tanks are empty and there is no delivery date. By evening your group needs to bring food back from a farm, get a neighbor to a scheduled appointment, and pick up a borrowed tool.

**Start with.** One car with four fuel tokens, two bikes, and a wagon. A round trip to the farm costs three tokens by car, the appointment costs two, and the tool pickup costs one. Bikes and the wagon cost a player their action for the round and can reach the farm or tool pickup. The neighbor needs a seated ride.

**Rounds.** Morning calls → midday trips → evening handoff. The appointment falls at midday.

**Twist.** A second household offers to share a ride and carry one load if your group can agree on a pickup point by midday.

**Bounty.** Confirm the seated ride, account for the food and tool, and leave a trip log that shows how much fuel remains and who still needs a ride.

## Trapped in One Square Mile

**Read aloud.** A creek overtops the approaches to your neighborhood. For the next 24 hours, the group and its supplies are inside a one-square-mile boundary. For an indoor version, replace the neighborhood with a community center and the streets with hallways.

**Start with.** Mark four locations or rooms. Put food for two shared meals in one, six sleeping spots in a second, and a notice board in a third. The fourth holds a household that has missed the first check-in. Add one access need and one useful offer at each location.

**Rounds.** First check-in → evening → next morning. Each player can visit one additional location per round.

**Twist.** Two people arrive at the sleeping area after a room becomes unusable in the story. They bring clean blankets and can cover a morning check-in if they have a place to rest.

**Bounty.** Hear from all four locations, find a place for everyone to eat and rest, and name someone to follow up on each outstanding need.

## The Tap Runs Dry

**Read aloud.** The water utility pauses service for repairs. Your group is organizing a shared meal while neighbors are calling to ask where they can get drinking water.

**Start with.** Ten fictional water tokens. Each of three households needs two each round. Cooking the meal costs two once. A nearby distribution point offers six more tokens, which two players can bring back together in one round. Each token stands for an in-game allotment of confirmed drinking water.

**Rounds.** First requests → meal setup → evening distribution.

**Twist.** A fourth household joins with the same needs going forward and can offer two people to help with collection. Adjust the meal or negotiate another delivery in the story if the ledger falls short. The facilitator gives any new deliveries a clear arrival time.

**Bounty.** Keep a public ledger showing what arrived and where it went. Confirm the next handoff and name any household with an outstanding request.

## The Harvest Comes In at Once

**Read aloud.** A farm has twelve crates of produce available today. The next buyer canceled, and the farm needs the space by tomorrow morning. Several neighbors want food and have offered to help.

**Start with.** Two cargo bikes that can carry one crate each per trip, a minivan that can carry four crates per trip, shared kitchen capacity for three crates, and storage space for four. One trip takes one player's action. Decide who can receive food at each destination before sending it.

**Rounds.** Take stock → afternoon pickups → last handoff before closing.

**Twist.** The kitchen can take only one crate today. Its volunteers offer to staff a pickup table if the group can find a place and a host.

**Bounty.** Find a confirmed use or storage place for as many crates as possible. Account for all twelve in the handoff and agree with the grower on what happens to any still on the farm.

## Pass it forward

Write a short record with your scenario, decisions, promises, outstanding needs, and one rule you would change. Include a drawing or a resource ledger if the next group can use it. Save the record with the played scenario in that group's repository, alongside a link back to the previous generation's repository or its portable archive. A later group can replay the starting scenario or begin with your open question.
